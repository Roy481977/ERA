# Sprint 023 — Painterly Trees (foliage Priority 3, solved)

**Status: EXECUTED (delivered; PROPOSED pending Roy). Kind: OPERATIONAL.
Simulation untouched — one function rebuilt, every tree inherits.**

## What was built

**The tree was rebuilt, not retinted.** Each tree now grows from a
seeded recipe: seven to nine jittered, flattened lobes whose colour
rises through a six-step lime-to-forest gradient — deep forest greens
in the body, warm lime at the crown where the light lives — over a
dark underside lobe that gives every canopy its own contained shade.
Three canopy species (round, tall-oval, spreading), seeded size
variation, two trunk tones, and per-tree palette shifts mean no two
trees on a street read identical.

**The oak keeps its nine-lobe canon form** but joins the gradient:
forest-deep body, lime light on the south-east lobes, and a broad
shadow mass beneath the crown.

**Shrubs go two-tone** (forest base, lime cap). **Five copses** of
three mixed trees settle the near slopes and field edges, so the
foliage reads as a population, not survivors.

Everything remains lobes and spheres — the Museum's cut-not-moulded
botany, repainted with light.

## Success criteria, answered

Volumetric painterly clusters — the underside-shadow-plus-gradient
makes each canopy read lit from above with depth, at every hour.
Varied canopy sizes — seeded size, species and palette per tree.
Warm lime-to-forest gradients — the six-step CANOPY ramp. Soft-edged
silhouettes — lobe overlap at higher segment counts; edges read
clustered, not conical. Before/after: the old trees are dark billiard
clusters; the new green's mouth pair alone shows the whole gradient.

## Honest notes

The gradient mutes at dusk when trees are backlit (physically right,
noted). Leaf texture remains out of scope by canon — ERA's botany is
lobed colour, not sprite foliage; if Roy wants micro-texture it should
enter as a Museum study first.
