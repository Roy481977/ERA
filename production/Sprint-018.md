# Sprint 018 — Continuous Fronts, Enclosed Blocks (Priority 3, solved completely)

**Status: EXECUTED (delivered; PROPOSED pending Roy). Kind: OPERATIONAL.
Simulation untouched — all fabric is viewer-layer; one micro-place (the
pub wall) moved beside the pub's front where its lean-crowd belongs.**

## What was built

**The north wall of Market Street.** Three new cottages (N1–N3) and a
corner house at the square (W1) join P03 and the pub in one continuous
row along the street's north side. Link sections with shared walls,
roofs and chimneys attach P03→N1, N2→N3 and W1→pub; the street now has
two unbroken building walls facing each other, with the green's mouth as
its one deliberate opening.

**The twitten.** The gap between N1 and N2 became a walled pedestrian
passage from Market Street to the gardens behind — 1.6 m of paving
between capped walls. From the street, it frames the railway and the
stopped train like a postcard.

**The square, walled on three sides.** North: the W1–pub row. West: the
corner house's gable continued by a capped garden wall down to the
street. South: a low stone wall with its gap at the bus stop, joining
the shelter. The east stays open — that is the Bridge Road reveal, and
it is canon.

**Two courtyards from dead space.** The pub's beer garden: a
brick-walled yard east of the pub with benches, an umbrella and its own
paving, entered from the square. The bakery's work yard: walls, crates
and flour sacks at the school corner, where the district's most-used
building shows its working side to School Lane.

**A charm correction.** The threshold bloom bushes had grown into a
gumball parade along the dense rows — reduced, paired with a green
companion shrub, and thinned to three houses in five.

## Success criteria, answered

**"Main thoroughfares flanked by contiguous building masses with shared
wall boundaries."** Market Street's south wall: bakery→stores→café→post
→terrace, all linked (Sprint 015). Its north wall, new: P03→N1→twitten→
N2→N3, then W1→pub. Every join is a physical link section sharing walls
with both neighbours.

**"Public spaces have clearly defined architectural edges on at least
three sides."** The square: built row north, gable-and-wall west, walled
south with the shelter — three defined edges, the fourth deliberately
open to the bridge. The green (its plateau walls, homes north, cottages
west, shops across its mouth) already exceeds three.

**"Empty negative space converted into functional courtyards or
enclosed pedestrian pathways."** The pub yard and bakery yard are
courtyards with purpose (evening overflow beside the lean-wall; the
bakery's deliveries); the twitten is an enclosed pedestrian pathway.
The last unassigned pocket on the street — between N3 and the pub — is
now W1's garden behind the square wall.

## Honest notes

The new cottages are viewer-layer fabric (same as Ground Row) — homes
without households until Roy schedules the population change. Nobody
routes through the twitten yet; it needs one walk-graph edge when the
sim next opens. The beer garden reads plain against the pub's blank east
wall — a climbing rose or window would fix it in a charm pass.
