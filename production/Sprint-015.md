## Gap analysis

`gap-analysis-concept-A.md` — ten ranked structural differences, each with

## What was built

**The landscape.** A ring of soft hills closes every horizon; hedgerow
lines and three distant roofs sit on the slopes; and along the northern
ridge runs the railway — embankment, catenary, and a stopped red train
with a cream band. The Book's railway is finally visible from the village.
Every wide shot now has foreground, middle ground and backdrop.

**The roofscape.** Warm pantile is now the sky of the village (two warm
tones over a slate minority), chimneys grew tall with capped stacks and
double pots. The silhouette is a rhythm of gables and chimneys, not a band.

**The terraces.** Link sections with their own roofs, chimneys and windows
join the south row and North Lane into continuous ensembles — the street
wall is now actually attached, gap by gap, like fabric.

**The river's arm.** The river re-routes north of the bridge to wrap the
pitch's west and north sides — the ground sits inside the bend, bank trees
follow the curve, and the canonical bridge crossing is untouched. From the
square the composition now reads: street → bridge → water → pitch →
hills — the concept's depth-stack in ERA's own geography.

**The bending street.** Bridge Road is now a real curved carriageway
sweeping from the square to the bridge (verged, like the lanes), continuing
as a gravel path over the bridge deck to the turnstiles.

**The furnished square.** Two market stalls under striped canvas (red and
teal), café umbrellas and a table at the pub's front, warm cobbled paving.
The square carries evidence of use seven days a week.

**The thresholds.** Homes now share one threshold grammar, seeded between
white picket fence (with a gate gap at the door) and the garden wall, each
with a bloom bush — the concept's public/private ritual in ERA's language.
Wall tints warmed: butter, cream and blush are the default; white render is
now the accent.

## Verification
