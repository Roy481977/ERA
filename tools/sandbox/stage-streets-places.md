## The six streets

**School Lane** (was west-lane). The children's street. The zebra with its
belisha beacons is its heart; term-time 08:45 and 15:05 are its rush hours,
the record's "children arrive at school" line made physical. Garden walls
low enough to see over. *Physical cues:* the crossing, the school green
beyond, chalk on the flags. *Sim record:* the teachers' and pupils' anchored
route; quiet in holidays — the street audibly keeps term.

**Green Row** (was green-south). The slow edge. Faces the green and the oak;
benches under trees; the worn cut to the oak leaves from here. Where
reconciliations walk (the record's quiet-place mending). *Cues:* longest
sightline in town, no shop signs, the oak's crown over the rooflines.

**Market Street** (was high-street). The engine. Bakery, café, stores, post
office in one painted run (CD-022); the pillar box and the phone box; Friday
stalls striped red-and-white; matchday bunting in club green. Its morning is
the town's morning — 90% of the bakery's life before 11:00. *Cues:* awnings,
fat signs, street lights, the only crowded pavement.

**North Lane** (was north-lane). The quiet lane. June's teal door; washing
lines (the trace record's washing out/in lives here); brick pavement, no
lamps. Retired walkers take it west every dry morning. *Cues:* gardens
deeper than front walls, one tree, no signage at all.

**The Loop** (was south-loop + links). The perimeter. Cyclists and
commuters; the field's edge; where the town thins out. *Cues:* wide, kerbed
both sides, hedges not walls.

**Bridge Road** (NEW name for the market→bridge→ground route). The matchday
river. Empty six days, a single direction on the seventh — the record shows
the 12:35 convergence and the 15:00 pour-back. Street lights the whole way
(evening matches will come). *Cues:* the bridge parapets, the pylons ahead,
the pub at its mouth catching the returning tide.

## Canonical place designs (form ← behaviour)

**The bakery.** Corner plot, mustard awning, window band the full frontage —
the shop window faces the street so the queue can see in and the baker can
see the queue (queues form outside, to the right of the door, as the local
layer already stages them). Step worn at the door. *It is the town's
introduction bureau (203 first meetings) — the door must open onto the
pavement, not a forecourt.*

**The café.** Orange awning, two window tables visible from the street
(quiet meetings need witnesses to feel safe). Deep enough for the corner
nobody overhears.

**The stores.** Navy fascia, goods spilling one crate onto the pavement
(passing trade; buy-because-passing is a recorded behaviour — incidental
intent completion). The phone box stands just beyond it.

**The pub.** Green trim, windows glowing before the lamps come on; a
stand-outside wall facing Bridge Road — it orients toward matchday traffic
(the record: 25–30 at five o'clock post-match, bigger after losses). Bench
outside for the lingerers.

**The post office.** Red fascia, pillar box outside, one window — the
errand place; its queue is indoors and patient.

**The green + the oak.** The emotional centre (Sheet 05 held). The oak is
THE landmark: two-lobed crown over everything, two benches beneath, the worn
paths arriving from three directions like spokes. Nothing else on the green
taller than a person.

**The allotments.** Gate as threshold (the latch click is the ritual);
sheds one per few plots, felt roofs; rows readable from North Lane. Where
peace gets made (12 reconciliations) — paths between beds wide enough for
two to walk slowly.

**The ground.** Roofed stand, club rail, two turnstiles (the pinch that
makes a crowd a queue and a queue a conversation), four pylons lit only when
it matters. The apron is where children play within sight of the pitch.

**The bridge.** Parapets at leaning height. The one door between the town
and its Saturdays.

**The bus shelter.** The covered wait; commuters' 09:00 and 17:00 stand
here — the one place strangers stand together daily (the familiar-stranger
fix will live here one day).

## Landmarks & homes

"Meet me by the oak" works today. The set: the oak, the bridge, the pillar
box, the phone box, the allotment gate, the bus shelter, the floodlights.
PROPOSED additions needing Roy's ruling: a war memorial on the green's west
edge (a place to stand still on one day a year) and a clock on the post
office fascia (the town's shared time — people meet "under the clock").

Homes are recognisable by accent + garden within the SL-6 family: June's
teal door on North Lane, Bill's red-doored house by the post office, Roy's
teal-trimmed lodging on the Loop. The viewer draws every home from its plot
data; no two are alike, all are one family.

## How the stage reinforces the simulation (the Phase 10 test, per decision)

Named streets → the historian can say "words on Market Street" (place
identity gains an address). Windows lit by occupancy → evidence of life with
zero new mechanics. Kerbs/zebra/shelter → the walking layer's routes read as
chosen, not algorithmic. The turnstile pinch → queue behaviour the local
layer already produces gets architecture that explains it. Bunting + pylons
lit → matchday transforms the town visibly before a single resident moves.
Worn paths drawn only where the record walks → the ground is a memory.
Every one of these amplifies an existing system; none adds one.
