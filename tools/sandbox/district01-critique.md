# DISTRICT 01 — the brutal critique (written before design, per the brief)

Compared against the target: a real English football village you could
sketch from memory. Ten weaknesses, ranked. Urbanistic, not stylistic.

**1. The village is a line, not a place.** Everything hangs off one 130-metre
spine. There is no knot, no crossroads, no centre of gravity. Ask someone to
sketch it and they draw a road with objects beside it. Villages are knots;
this is a corridor. This is *the* problem — most of the rest follows from it.

**2. There is no enclosure anywhere.** The street is ~6.5 m of carriageway
with buildings set back 8–12 m on the north and nothing at all on most of
the south. Width-to-height ratio ≈ 10:1. A village street is 2:1 to 4:1.
Nothing ever stands close enough to anything else to make a wall, so every
photograph is mostly ground and sky. This is why it feels assembled.

**3. The green is a traffic verge, not a village green.** A 28×8 m strip
pinched between the road and the shops' setback. No building faces into it;
it has no back edge, no room shape. The oak — the town's cathedral — stands
in a corridor between a kerb and a shopfront.

**4. The shops don't hold the street.** They float 9 m behind their own
pavement, each in its own pool of space. A shop row is a wall with doors;
ours is a row of pavilions.

**5. Housing is not fabric.** Ten homes in four disconnected strips at
roughly one house per 400 m². Nothing shares a boundary except where last
iteration forced hedges between the gaps. There is no terrace rhythm, no
paired cottages, no cluster — so there is no village body, only outbuildings
of an invisible centre.

**6. The square is a leftover, not a destination.** A bald patch between
the pub and the bus stop, defined by nothing on three sides. It was dressed
last iteration (flag, planters, noticeboard) — decoration on a void, which
is exactly what the failure conditions describe.

**7. No vertical accent, no skyline.** Every building is one storey plus a
roof. The village has no silhouette, nothing to orient by, nothing for the
eye to find from the bridge or the green. (A clock over the post office has
been PROPOSED canon since Phase 10 and never enacted.)

**8. Streets never turn and vistas never close.** Every view runs to
horizon. Not one street ends on a building face, the oak, or the clock.
Closed vistas are how villages photograph themselves; we have none.

**9. Football is invisible six days in seven.** Bunting and pennants are
matchday-only; the ground hides beyond the river. A stranger walking the
village on a Tuesday would not discover that football is the centre of this
society — the brief says they must.

**10. The east end dribbles out.** Between the pub and the bridge the
village just stops. The arrival from the bridge — the away supporter's
first sight — has no village front, no gateway.

## The diagnosis

Weaknesses 1–6 are one weakness: **the plan**. No amount of trees, props,
lighting or material work touches them — last iteration proved it by doing
everything except the plan, beautifully, and the district still reads as a
prototype. The fix is to redraw the plan: pull the buildings to the street,
wrap the green in fronts, form the fabric, and let the ground do the rest.

**Order of attack this iteration: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7 (the clock), 6 (the
square gains its pub wall). Deferred: 8 (a true street bend needs diagonal
road support in the drawing layer), 9 (a permanent club presence in the
village belongs with the resident redesign), 10 (the gateway wants its own
iteration).**
