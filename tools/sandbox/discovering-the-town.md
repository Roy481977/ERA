## What moved, and why (the record justified every move)

**ADJ-7 — the terrace.** P14 (Mabel, Joe, Wyn) and P15 (Jack's lodging) leave
the Loop and face the shops across Market Street. The street the eye-level
camera showed as one-sided is now a corridor: post office and stores on one
side, two garden-walled houses on the other, the phone box and lamps between.
Jack — the newcomer who adopted the pub within weeks — now lives thirty
metres from it.

**BenchB moves under the oak.** The record shows benchB as the quiet seat
(reconciliations, solitary sitters); it now sits inside the oak's shade where
that behaviour already lived. BenchA stays facing the street — the watching
seat. Two benches, two personalities, both earned.

**ADJ-6 — footpaths.** The Market Street front-door spine, the Loop link at
P13, and the North Lane cut now exist as designed paths; the residents used
these lines all year as grass shortcuts — the ground now admits it.

**ADJ-11 — the riverside path.** Square → riverbank → bridge. The dullest
walk in town (bus shelter → café, straight across dead lawn) gains a bend,
water, and a reason.

**ADJ-9 — the apron shrunk** by a third; the ground reads nearer the town.

**ADJ-12 — worn paths from the record.** The desire lines drawn on the green
are now generated from the year's actual place-to-place movement (top
transitions involving the green, oak, benches, field and allotments). The
ground is literally a memory.

**Concealment.** Three framing trees stand where Bridge Road leaves the
square: from the town the pylons are hidden; walking east they appear in
stages — tops, heads, then the whole ground past the bridge. Hedgerows now
line the Loop, so the south row reads held rather than floating.

## Place personalities (why come · why stay · why leave · what changes)

**The bakery.** Come for bread before work; stay because the queue faces the
street and the window is warm; leave when the shift bell of your own day
rings. Rain packs the queue inside the door; matchday adds scarves by ten;
winter makes the window the brightest thing on the street; after sunset it
is dark and everyone knows it sleeps early. *Gather ✓ memories ✓ (203 first
meetings) wait ✓ observe ✓ hide ✗ 07:00≠19:00 ✓ screenshot-recognisable ✓.*

**The green & oak.** Come because three routes cross it; stay for the shade
and the benches; leave when the light goes. Rain empties it in minutes;
matchday drains it east; winter cuts it to the mown path; sunset hands it to
the teenagers' edge. *Gather ✓ memories ✓ (June & Petra's bench) wait ✓
observe ✓ (benchA) hide ✓ (benchB, under the crown) 07:00≠19:00 ✓
recognisable ✓ — the strongest space in town.*

**The pub.** Come for the evening or the result; stay for the third pint and
the wall outside; leave at closing or when the argument starts. Rain fills
it; a loss fills it harder (the record's truth); winter gives it the warmest
windows; sunset is its opening bell. *All eight ✓ except hide.*

**The market square.** Come Friday or don't; stay while the stalls stand;
leave when they fold. The riverside path now gives it a second life as the
turn in an evening walk. *Its weekday emptiness is honest — a square that
breathes weekly, not daily.*

**The ground.** Come only when it matters — that is its whole personality.
The railing holds the waiting; the stand holds the ninety minutes; the
bridge holds the anticipation both ways. Off-days it belongs to the
groundsman and the children on the apron. *Memories ✓ (June's 26 of 26)
wait ✓ observe ✓ different-by-hour ✓✓.*

**The bus shelter.** The one roof strangers share daily. Come at 09:00 and
17:00; stay eight minutes; leave together without speaking. Rain makes it
the most crowded place in town per square metre.
