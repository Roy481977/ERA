# THE STAGE GRAMMAR — Phase 10

**Status: PROPOSED. Consolidates existing canon (Visual DNA v0.2, Museum SL-1..7,
CD-022 the painted street, CD-023 cut-not-moulded, CD-024 baseline, CD-025
evidence of life, CD-026 space is the unit, CD-027 the Weave). Where this
document repeats the Museum it cites it; where it extends it, the extension is
marked NEW and awaits review.**

The physical world is now canon, held to the same discipline as behaviour,
football, institutions, memory and relationships. Every object class below
carries two clauses: its FORM (which Museum law it obeys) and its BEHAVIOUR
(what it invites, reveals, or remembers — the Phase 10 test). An object with
no behaviour clause does not enter production.

---

## 1 · Architecture

**Walls & brick** (Museum 08). Brick 60 / paint 25 / stone 15 (SL-6 share).
Brick family BRK (200,100,72), BRK2 (160,70,58), BRK3 (216,164,120); paint
family: chalk cream, butter, apricot, dusty rose, sage, powder blue, ash plum,
warm stone — all warm-leaning, none primary, no teal on walls (CD-04).
*Behaviour:* the paint beat (butter, brick, sage, brick) is how a resident
knows their own street in fog; homes become recognisable before doors do.

**Roof forms** (Museum 05). Two tiles only: grey-blue slate TILG (134,142,160)
and pantile TILB (200,128,90), roughly 2:1 across town. Ridge reads as a
thick line (SL-2: RTHICK 0.225). One eaves overhang town-wide (SL-7).
*Behaviour:* the roofline is the town's silhouette from Bridge Road — the
walk home is navigated by it.

**Windows** (SL-2/SL-7). One module: 1.04 × 1.44, frames visibly thick, glass
GLS (98,128,150) cold when a room is empty, GLSI (214,190,146) warm when
lived-in. *Behaviour:* NEW — windows are the town's occupancy display.
A lit window is evidence of life (CD-025) that costs nothing and reads at
every distance. The viewer already implements this.

**Doors.** One door height (SL-7). Door colour is the household's accent —
the one loud element each house is allowed (calm in materials spends its
whole budget here). *Behaviour:* "the teal door on North Lane" is how you
give directions to June's house.

**Chimneys.** One per roof, brick, offset toward the party wall.
*Behaviour:* smoke is a future evidence-of-life channel (cold mornings,
who's home) — the form reserves the slot.

## 2 · Ground

**Roads** — warm charcoal asphalt (SL-4: walked off blue-black), and a road
meets a KERB, never grass (SL-5: kerb ribbon +0.34 under every carriageway).
**Pavements** — flags on shopping streets, brick on lanes, one width (SL-7).
**Worn paths** (CD-025) — soil-toned desire lines; they are RECORDS: a worn
path exists only where the simulation actually walks. *Behaviour:* the
shortcut across the green teaches new residents where everyone goes.

**Drainage** — gully at each kerb corner. *Behaviour:* rain has an address;
puddles form where the ground says, and walkers step around them (Phase 9).

## 3 · Green things

Trees: 5-lobed crowns, cut not moulded; one big unique (THE oak, s×1.35).
Shrubs half-height; flowers only in front gardens and allotment rows (colour
budget: they borrow the household accent). *Behaviour:* trees are shade in
summer, shelter in rain, and the oak is the town's "meet me by" (landmark
law below).

## 4 · Street furniture

Benches — timber, seat 2, visibly thick (SL-2); placed with sight of a
route, never in a void (a bench beneath a tree invites lingering; a slightly
hidden bench creates solitude). Bus shelter — the covered wait; TILG roof.
Signs — fat boards, serif capitals, one per shopfront. Bins by school gate
and ground. Pillar box RED near the post office; phone box RED by the
stores — both are meeting points, not props. Street lights — Market Street
and Bridge Road only (the lanes stay dark; the dark is a texture too), warm
at dusk. *Behaviour:* every furniture item is a waiting spot, and waiting is
life (Phase 9's waiting law).

## 5 · Football infrastructure

Pitch with true markings; roofed stand with the club-green rail; two
turnstiles (the queue is the point); four floodlight pylons, 4-lamp heads —
lit only on home matchdays, visible from every street (Football Is
Geography). Club colours (GRN + white) appear as: the stand rail, matchday
bunting on Market Street shops, supporters' scarf rings. *Behaviour:* the
ground influences the district with no match on — the pylons are the
compass; children kick balls where the apron meets the field.

## 6 · The one rule over all of it

SL-7 — order is a material. One door height, one storey, one kerb, one
pavement width, one window module. Variation lives in placement, colour and
combination; never in dimension. This is what keeps a procedurally built
town from looking procedurally built.
