# THE ENCLOSED VILLAGE — visual iteration under the Continuous Directive

**Status: PROPOSED.** The simulation is untouched — same year, same lives,
same record. Everything below is spatial composition in the presentation
layer, per the directive's priority: composition before asset polish.

---

## The reading of the brief

The directive names the milestone — a believable English football village —
and names the gap: spatial composition, density, hierarchy, edges, rooms.
Phase 13 made the town photograph well; this iteration makes it *hold
together*. The single organising idea: **the village gains edges.** A
village is not buildings on a plain; it is a piece of countryside enclosed
by hedgerow, fence, verge and tree until it feels kept.

## What was composed

**Street hierarchy.** Market Street keeps its urban dignity — kerbed, paved,
full width. Every other road stopped pretending to be a street: School
Lane, North Lane and the Loop are now narrower, warmer-toned carriageways
with grass verges where the kerbs were. Roads no longer dominate; lanes
read as lanes.

**The hedgerow and fence network.** A post-and-rail fence runs the length
of the playing field's road edge, broken by two gates where the record says
people actually enter. The allotments hide behind a full hedgerow with one
entrance. North Lane's cottages sit behind a garden hedge with three gate
gaps. Shrub clusters soften the riverside bend. The district's spaces now
have named edges — and gaps in edges are where life enters.

**Tree composition.** School Lane became an avenue — three facing pairs,
the district's first formal tree statement, framing the walk south to the
field. The green's two southern corners gained corner trees, closing the
room around the oak without walling it. An orchard of eight small fruit
trees stands past the allotments, softening the district's northern edge.
Market Street itself gained its rhythm: five front-garden trees along the
south side, so the shopping street finally has a green counter-beat to the
shopfronts.

**Garden integration.** Dividing hedges now stand between neighbouring
homes on North Lane, Green Row and the terrace. Four in ten houses grew a
back-garden shed, placed off the house's actual road-facing (the same logic
that hangs the washing behind). Gardens overlap; plots stop floating.

**The green as a room.** Daisy drifts in three patches, a flowerbed beside
the watching bench, a bin where a bin belongs. Small things — but the green
now reads as *kept* ground, not surplus lawn.

**Football is geography.** Two white kickabout goals stand permanently on
the playing field — the children's football answered in the landscape, not
just in behaviour. On home Saturdays every supporter household hangs a
green pennant at eave height by the front door, the square's new flagpole
flies the club flag, and the bunting does the rest. On a matchday you can
now trace support house by house.

**The square dressed.** Flagpole, two planters, and a parish noticeboard —
the square's weekday emptiness stays honest (canon), but the room itself is
furnished.

## Verification (what the honest pass caught)

The first build of the matchday house pennants floated at head height on
poles too thin to render — remounted on the house fronts at eave height,
where an English house flag actually lives. A verification camera aimed
with the wrong axis sign briefly misdiagnosed the green as a broken field —
worth recording: check the camera before the geometry.

## Canon review — the four categories

**Preserved Canon.** The entire simulation and record; Market Street's
kerbed urban character (the hierarchy only works if one street stays
urban); the green's open south face to the street (a green is not a
courtyard); the square's weekday emptiness (CD: honest weekly breathing);
every Phase 13 hero; the Museum palette and cut-not-moulded forms
throughout — every new object is a box, a sphere, or a cone in existing
colours.

**Strengthened Canon.** The worn paths now cross a green with corners,
flowers and daisies — the memory-ground reads stronger inside a room.
The Phase-12 micro-places gain context: the teens' corner sits by a real
fence line, the bikes lean outside shops on a street with trees. The
laundry system's road-facing logic was generalised (sheds, flags reuse it) —
one rule, three behaviours, which is how canon should grow.

**New Canon Candidates.** (1) *The edge grammar*: fence = productive/active
land (field), hedge = tended land (allotments, gardens), verge = lane,
kerb = street. If ratified, District 02 inherits it as data. (2) *The
supporter's house pennant* — football support made legible at the front
door, matchday only. (3) *The avenue as street signature*: one formal tree
statement per district, everything else informal. (4) *Kickabout goals* —
permanent children's football furniture on any play field.

**Rejected Directions.** Ground-planted flag poles by front doors (read as
floating markers at eye level — rejected for wall mounts). Hedging the
green's street edge (tried mentally, rejected: the green must spill toward
the street; the low wall is already the right amount of edge). Kerbing the
lanes (rejected: uniform kerbs were exactly why roads dominated).

## Creative Director review

**What became stronger?** The middle distance. Before, every eye-level
frame ended in lawn; now it ends in a fence line, a hedge, an avenue, or
a goal. The district photographs as *somewhere* from almost any angle,
which is the directive's camera test.

**What became weaker?** Nothing removed; but the density gradient now
exposes the west end of Market Street (x < 36) as the thinnest stretch —
the new trees stop short of the school corner and the zebra feels alone.

**What still feels generic?** The residents (unchanged this iteration, by
design — composition first). The shop interiors behind glass. The river
banks, which are still two colours meeting in a line.

**What still feels procedural?** The allotment soil rows (perfectly
parallel), and the field's mown stripes read mechanical beside the new
informal edges.

**What would visitors remember?** The avenue on School Lane; the goal
beyond the fence with children at it; the flag over the square on a
Saturday; the oak's room now that it has corners.

**Weakest emotional location?** The bus shelter — the one roof strangers
share daily is still a grey box on a pole, with no bench, no timetable, no
worn ground beneath it. It deserves the next micro-pass.

**Next major redesign?** The residents themselves — faces, hands,
silhouette variety. The stage is now composed enough that the players are
visibly the weakest thing on it. (Phase 13's critique said it; this
iteration confirms it.)
