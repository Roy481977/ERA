# GAP ANALYSIS — District 01 vs `concept-A-village-green.png`

Written before any implementation, per the sprint brief. Structural
differences only, ranked by how much of the "same game?" verdict each one
carries. The concept is not copied; its qualities are extracted.

---

**1. The concept sits in a landscape; District 01 floats on a lawn.**
*Why the concept succeeds:* rolling hills close every sightline, the
village nestles in a valley, and a railway with a passing train runs along
the ridge — the village is a place inside a larger world, and every frame
has foreground, middle ground and backdrop.
*Why District 01 fails:* an infinite flat plane; the horizon is a fog line;
beyond the last fence there is nothing, so wide shots read as a diorama on
green paper.
*Redesign:* a ring of soft hill masses to the north and east, hedgerow
lines and a few distant roofs on the slopes, and the railway embankment
with a stopped train on the northern ridge — the Book's railway, finally
visible. (Largest single lever in the whole comparison.)

**2. The concept's sky is made of roofs and chimneys; ours is flat.**
*Why the concept succeeds:* steep red pantile roofs at slightly varied
heights, and a chimney at every party wall — the skyline is a rhythm; warm
terracotta is the village's colour before any wall is seen.
*Why District 01 fails:* low grey-blue roofs dominate, chimneys are small
and rare, so the silhouette is a flat band and the aerial reads cool.
*Redesign:* shift the roof mix decisively to warm pantile, raise and
thicken every chimney, two pots each — the skyline becomes a beat.

**3. The concept is terraced; District 01 is detached.**
*Why the concept succeeds:* houses touch — continuous terraces with shared
rooflines make walls of buildings, which is what makes streets read as
rooms and the village read dense while staying tiny.
*Why District 01 fails:* even after Sprint 014's street wall, every
building is a free-standing object with daylight between; the gaps say
"assets", not "fabric".
*Redesign:* attach the rows — infill link sections with continuous roofs
between neighbours on the south row and North Lane, so each row becomes one
ensemble with a shared roofline.

**4. The concept's river bends and holds the pitch in its arm.**
*Why the concept succeeds:* the river curves around the foreground, the
pitch with its stand and floodlights sits inside the bend, and the stone
bridge is the hinge between village and football — geography *is* the
football story.
*Why District 01 fails:* the river is a near-straight strip and the ground
sits beyond it on an open apron, visually unrelated to the water or town.
*Redesign:* re-route the river to sweep east around the ground's south
side so the pitch sits inside the bend, line the banks with trees, and keep
the bridge exactly where canon fixed it (the crossing point is unchanged).

**5. The concept's square is furnished and alive; ours is paved and dressed.**
*Why the concept succeeds:* clock tower, striped market stalls, café
umbrellas and chairs — the square carries evidence of daily use at all
times; it is the composition's heart.
*Why District 01 fails:* the square has a flag, planters and a noticeboard
on empty paving — dressing on a void six days in seven.
*Redesign:* permanent stall frames with striped canvas, a café terrace of
umbrellas and tables at the pub's edge, warm cobble-toned paving.

**6. The concept's streets bend; ours are drawn with a set square.**
*Why the concept succeeds:* roads curve and fork around blocks; closed
vistas and natural asymmetry come free.
*Why District 01 fails:* every road is an axis-aligned rectangle.
*Redesign this sprint:* the Bridge Road sweep — a real curved carriageway
from the square to the bridge (the walk already bends; the road now
follows). Full curved-street support for the core grid is named for a
future sprint, not faked now.

**7. The concept has one threshold grammar; ours has three.**
*Why the concept succeeds:* every cottage shows the same tiny ritual —
picket fence, gate gap, hedge, one bright shrub — so public and private
space have a legible, repeated boundary.
*Why District 01 fails:* some homes have garden walls, some hedges, some
nothing; the boundary language is inconsistent, so it reads procedural.
*Redesign:* unify — white picket fence with a gate gap at every home
front, hedge behind, one bloom bush; walls remain only on Market Street
(urban edge).

**8. The concept layers depth in every direction; ours in one.**
*Why the concept succeeds:* from any angle there is something near (pitch,
river), something middle (streets), something far (hills, train) — the
tilt-shift look is really a depth composition.
*Why District 01 fails:* depth exists only along the street axis; from
north or east the frame is building-then-void.
*Redesign:* covered by 1 + 4 (hills behind, river-and-pitch foreground) —
the check is that *every* compass direction gains a background.

**9. Warmth is the concept's default; ours is an accent.**
*Why the concept succeeds:* butter, cream and blush walls under terracotta,
warm grey cobbles — the coolest thing in frame is the river.
*Why District 01 fails:* white render and grey asphalt dominate; warmth
lives only in awnings and doors.
*Redesign:* wall-tint distribution warmed (more butter/cream/blush, less
white), the square cobbled warm; roads stay asphalt only where the street
hierarchy says urban.

**10. The concept's landmark is vertical and central; ours is peripheral.**
*Why the concept succeeds:* the clock tower stands at the square — the
village has one strong vertical you orient by from everywhere.
*Why District 01 fails:* our clock (Sprint 014) sits on the post office
mid-street — right for the street vista, invisible from the square and
green.
*Redesign:* keep the post office clock (street-scale accent) and let the
skyline (2) plus floodlights carry verticality this sprint; a true square
campanile is proposed to Roy rather than built unbidden — it is a canon
question (the concept's tower vs our street clock), flagged for ruling.

## Order of attack

1 (landscape) → 2 (roofscape) → 3 (terraces) → 4 (river bend) → 5 (square
life) → 6 (bridge-road sweep) → 7 (thresholds). 8 falls out of 1+4; 9 rides
along 2/5/7; 10 goes to Roy.
